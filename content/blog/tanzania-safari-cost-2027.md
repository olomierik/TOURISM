The short answer: a private mid-range safari in northern Tanzania runs at roughly **US$230–470 per person per day**, and a large share of that is government fees that no operator can discount. <!-- VERIFY: ranges are the midrange bands in destination_costs, fees_as_of 2026 -->

This guide breaks that number down by park, by comfort level and by season, using the cost bands this site publishes for each destination and the prices operators have listed here. Where a figure needs checking before you rely on it, it says so.

## What one day on safari pays for

A quoted day rate is not one price but four:

- **Park fees**, paid per person per day to the park authority (TANAPA for most national parks, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority for the crater).
- **The vehicle and driver-guide**, usually a 4x4 Land Cruiser shared by your group.
- **Accommodation**, from a public campsite to a tented lodge.
- **Food, water and the operator's margin.**

The park fee is the part that does not move. Everything else is where budget, mid-range and luxury trips differ.

## When you travel changes the price

Lodges price by season. The dry months, roughly **June to October**, are peak season in the northern parks: game gathers at water, and rooms cost the most. **April and May** are the long rains, when many lodges discount heavily and some close. <!-- VERIFY: lodge seasonality is general knowledge, not site data -->

The site's month-by-month ratings for Tarangire, for example, give its wildlife five out of five from July to September and two out of five from December to April. A cheaper month is often cheaper for a reason.

## Private or shared

A private safari means the vehicle is yours. A shared (or "join-in") safari fills the seats with other travellers and splits the vehicle cost, which is the single biggest saving available after choosing camping over lodges. The trade-off is a fixed itinerary.
