Zanzibar eats differently in each part of the island. Stone Town is street food and old merchant houses, the north is beach restaurants built for sunsets, and the east coast around Paje is relaxed, kitesurf-town cooking. This guide goes area by area and names places listed on this site, so you can see their contact details and location before you go. None of them has a traveller review here yet, so it is a guide to where to look, not a ranking.

## Stone Town

Stone Town is where most visitors eat their first Zanzibari meal, and where the island's mix of Swahili, Omani, Indian and Persian cooking is easiest to taste.

- **Forodhani Gardens** turns into a food market on the waterfront every evening: grilled seafood, sugar-cane juice and "Zanzibar pizza", a stuffed, griddled flatbread.
- **Mercury's Restaurant**, on the waterfront, is named after Freddie Mercury, who was born in Stone Town.

The market is the cheap way to eat; the sit-down restaurants along the seafront cost more.

## Nungwi and Kendwa (the north)

The northern tip has the island's calmest swimming at all tides and the most sunset-facing restaurants. **Mama Mia Restaurant** is one of the places listed here.

## Paje, Jambiani and Michamvi (the east coast)

The east coast's tide goes out a long way, which shapes its restaurants: many are on the beach itself, and some are reachable on foot only at low tide.

- **The Rock**, at Michamvi Pingwe, is built on a coral outcrop in the lagoon; at high tide you reach it by boat.
- **Bounty Beach**, **Gusto** and **Eden Paradise** are among the places listed around Paje. Check that a place is open before you travel to it.

## Before you go

- Many smaller places take cash only, so carry some.
- During Ramadan, many places in Stone Town close in the daytime and open after sunset.
- Dress modestly away from the beach, especially in Stone Town.
