Zanzibar eats differently in each part of the island. Stone Town is street food and old merchant houses, the north is beach restaurants built for sunsets, and the east coast around Paje is relaxed, kitesurf-town cooking. This guide goes area by area and names places listed on this site, so you can see their contact details and location before you go.

## Stone Town

Stone Town is where most visitors eat their first Zanzibari meal, and where the island's mix of Swahili, Omani, Indian and Persian cooking is easiest to taste.

- **Forodhani Gardens** turns into a food market on the waterfront every evening: grilled seafood, sugar-cane juice and "Zanzibar pizza", a stuffed, griddled flatbread. <!-- VERIFY -->
- **Mercury's Restaurant**, on the waterfront, is named after Freddie Mercury, who was born in Stone Town. <!-- VERIFY -->

Expect to pay little at the market and mid-range prices at the sit-down restaurants along the seafront. <!-- VERIFY -->

## Nungwi and Kendwa (the north)

The northern tip has the island's calmest swimming at all tides and the most sunset-facing restaurants. **Mama Mia Restaurant** is one of the places listed here. <!-- VERIFY: area taken from the listing's address -->

## Paje, Jambiani and Michamvi (the east coast)

The east coast's tide goes out a long way, which shapes its restaurants: many are on the beach itself, and some are reachable on foot only at low tide.

- **The Rock**, at Michamvi Pingwe, is built on a coral outcrop in the lagoon; at high tide you reach it by boat. <!-- VERIFY -->
- **Bounty Beach**, **Gusto** and **Eden Paradise** are among the places listed around Paje. <!-- VERIFY: names exactly as listed; check each is still open -->

## Before you go

- Many smaller restaurants are cash only. <!-- VERIFY -->
- During Ramadan, many places in Stone Town close during the day and open after sunset. <!-- VERIFY -->
- Dress modestly away from the beach, especially in Stone Town. <!-- VERIFY -->
