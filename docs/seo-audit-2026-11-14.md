# Why impressions are not becoming clicks — audit, 14 November 2026

Sources: the `exploretanzania.online` Domain property in Search Console
(22 Oct – 10 Nov 2026), the production site measured directly, and the
production database's own query statistics. Every figure is measured.

**30 clicks · ~6,000 impressions · 0.5% CTR · average position 55.6**

## The answer

Two causes, and the second was not visible from Search Console at all.

### 1. The impressions are on page six, where nobody looks

| Where the page ranks | Pages | Impressions | Clicks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positions 1–10 | 336 | 1,068 | 14 |
| Positions 11–20 | 91 | 445 | 16 |
| Positions 21–50 | 140 | 723 | 1 |
| Below position 50 | 272 | **4,539** | 1 |

Two thirds of all impressions come from pages ranked below 50. An impression
there means Google listed the page on its sixth screen of results; it does not
mean a person saw it. The titles are not the problem — where the site does
reach the first two pages it earns 30 of its 31 page-level clicks.

By kind of page:

| Page type | Pages | Impressions | Clicks | Avg position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Category × destination | 241 | 3,215 | 5 | 63.0 |
| Destination guides | 127 | 1,113 | 0 | 62.1 |
| Operator listings | 350 | 1,040 | 8 | 16.7 |
| Travel guides | 38 | 684 | 2 | 72.7 |
| Category pages and other | 28 | 412 | 2 | 64.3 |
| Wildlife | 9 | 103 | 1 | 13.0 |
| When to go | 40 | 100 | 0 | 61.8 |
| Home | 2 | 95 | 14 | 14.3 |

Listings rank well (16.7) because the query is the operator's own name and
there is little competition for it. The pages built to win commercial searches
— "hotels in Serengeti", "safari operators in Arusha" — sit around 63.

### 2. The site has been slow and partly broken for crawlers

Measured against production on 14 November:

| Page | Result |
|---|---|
| `/hotels/mount-kenya` — the page with the most impressions | **504 timeout** after 31 s, twice |
| `/hotels/serengeti` | **504 timeout** |
| `/directory` | 200 after **35 s** |
| Home, already cached | first byte after **5.1 s** |

The cause is in the database. Every public table hanging off a listing decided
what a visitor may see by calling a function once per row, and Postgres cannot
optimise through that kind of function:

- the listing-card query had run **387,715 times at 502 ms each** — 54 hours
  of database time, with the next eleven slowest entries being the same query;
- counting listings took **17.4 seconds**, against a 3-second limit for
  anonymous visitors, so reads were being cancelled;
- when a read is cancelled the page falls back to placeholder content or
  times out.

Google ranks slow pages lower and crawls them less. A page that times out
cannot rank at all, and the one timing out was the single best opportunity in
the account (five Mount Kenya queries, 406 impressions).

This is also the likeliest explanation for part of AdSense's "low value
content" verdict: a reviewer, or its crawler, was being served placeholder
pages and timeouts. The existing analysis in `ADSENSE-CONTENT-AUDIT.md` —
thousands of stub listings with no description — is the other part.

## What was fixed

| Fix | Effect |
|---|---|
| Migration `090`: visibility rules rewritten so Postgres can plan them | Listing count 17,405 ms → 466 ms; destination links 446 ms → 4 ms; identical rows returned |
| Public data cached for an hour instead of five minutes | Pages nobody edited are rebuilt twelve times less often |
| Combination-page titles carry the listing count | "Hotels and lodges in Mount Kenya — compare N listings", N being the figure the page shows |
| Redundant `https://www.` Search Console property removed | One property, the Domain one, which covers every address |

Migration 090 was tested inside a transaction that was rolled back: a visitor
sees exactly the same rows in all ten tables, and a listing that is taken down
still disappears at once.

It was then applied to production, and the same pages measured again:

| Page | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `/hotels/mount-kenya` | 504 after 31 s | 200 in 3.3–5.8 s |
| `/hotels/serengeti` | 504 after 32 s | 200 in 5.6 s |
| `/directory` | 200 after 35 s | 200 in 3.2 s |
| `/de/reiseziele/cratere-du-ngorongoro` (French slug, German path) | 504 | 308 to `/de/reiseziele/ngorongoro-krater` |
| `/destinations/parc-national-du-serengeti` | 504 | 308 to `/destinations/serengeti` |
| Home | first byte 5.1 s | 2.3 s in total |

These times include about 1.3 s of network latency from where they were
measured, and the first request to each page was a fresh render. The cache
change and the titles need a deploy to take effect.

## Pages not indexed: 1,260

3,500 pages are indexed. The 1,260 that are not:

| Reason | Pages | What it is | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excluded by `noindex` | 543 | Operator pages in French, German, Italian and Swahili that have no translation, plus thin stubs. Deliberate: indexing them would publish near-duplicates | None. Correct as it stands |
| Blocked by robots.txt | 263 | 48 are `/api` addresses, correctly blocked. The rest were crawled on 30 October under an older robots.txt; today's allows them | Re-validate in Search Console once 090 is live |
| Alternate page with canonical | 147 | `www.` and query-string copies pointing at the real page | None. Working as designed |
| Not found (404) | 132 | Addresses mixing one language's path with another's slug, e.g. `/de/reiseziele/cratere-du-ngorongoro` | The redirect for these already exists in the code, but the pages were timing out before it could run. Fixed by 090 |
| Discovered, not indexed | 61 | Real pages Google has found and not yet fetched — it backs off from slow sites | Speeds up once the site is fast |
| Page with redirect | 41 | Old `www.` and parked-language addresses | None |
| Other 4xx | 33 | Same mixed-language addresses as the 404s | Fixed by 090 |
| Duplicate, Google chose another canonical | 9 | — | None |
| Server error (5xx) | 4 | The timeouts described above | Fixed by 090 |
| Redirect error | 1 | — | None |

So about **230 pages** (404 + 4xx + discovered + 5xx) are recoverable, and one
cause — the slow database — sits behind all of them. The sitemaps are healthy:
739 URLs submitted across nine files, all read successfully.

## For AI search

Already in place: `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt`, robots rules that admit
ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google's AI crawlers, and Organization,
WebSite, BreadcrumbList, ItemList and Offer structured data.

What AI answers quote is a specific fact with a source. The pages that can
supply those are `/trip-cost` (priced by destination), the when-to-go pages and
the wildlife guide. The work is the same as for Google: question-shaped
headings ("How much does a 5-day Serengeti safari cost?") with the figure in
the first sentence beneath.

## The $10,000-by-December target

Stated plainly, because planning around a number that cannot be reached costs
more than hearing it now.

- Premium is $120 a year and Featured is $200. $10,000 is **83 Premium or 50
  Featured subscriptions**.
- 34 listings have an owner today. Seven are on a paid plan or its trial.
- Search sends about 10 visitors a week.

Search traffic will not produce 80 paying operators in six weeks; rankings
move over months. The route that could produce revenue in that time does not
depend on Google at all:

- **255 operators received WhatsApp chats from this site in the last 30 days**
  — 805 chats. That is proof of value in a form an operator understands.
- Contact those 255 directly, busiest first, with their own number: "your
  listing started 23 WhatsApp conversations last month — claim it."
- Claiming starts the 60-day Premium trial that already exists.

A realistic December result is tens of claims and the first handful of paid
plans. Getting to $10,000 needs that pipeline running for a few months, with
search growing underneath it.
